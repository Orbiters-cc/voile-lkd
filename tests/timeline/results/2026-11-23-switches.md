**The same on a hot, busy Mac** (thermal pressure 2, as in the three VRChat starts, another program at a core and a half; 23:50 to 23:53): nothing 24.5
frames a second, 0 cuts; the event query 14.2, 5.8 cuts; the disjoint pair 15.4, 9.5 cuts, and 23.7, 5.0 cuts (pressure not noted); the rehearsal at 100 % 17.9 shown a
second, 8.4 cuts. The slower the commands are decoded and the longer the GPU takes to reach the query, the more looks fall into the
scene: it feeds itself. (Disturbed runs: for the mechanism, not for a size.)

So 1.6 to 3.6 ms a cut **on this client** (blended 2x content in every pixel: it compresses badly; the earlier flat scenes gave 0.39 ms).
At 58 % (7.3 ms of GPU a frame) the event query is there before 64 looks: 0.11 cuts, 136 against 137 frames a second.

**The stall flush.** Neptune's host polls every query that was ended every 100 us with `GetData(DONOTFLUSH)` (`npt_feedback.c`, for the guest's
shared-memory results). DXMT counts the looks at an event or disjoint query that is not there yet (`d3d11_query.hpp`, 64) and from then on
drops `DONOTFLUSH`: each look, every 100 us, flushes whatever has been recorded until the GPU gets to the query. 6.4 ms after such an `End`
and until it is signalled, every 100 us of decoded commands is a command buffer of its own. **It is in VRChat's recordings**: the time
between two command buffers of one queue is 100 to 120 us in 16,619 of 41,193 at 45 % (9,121 of 26,813 at 58 %, 8,662 of 26,796 at 100 %)
and below 100 us in 2,167 (1,685; 989): a wall at the poll's interval. 17.5, 14.3 and 24.0 command buffers a frame, two `Flush` a frame.

What a fix would be is in the report that goes with this; nothing of DXMT's rendering was changed. D3D11 asks for no flush here at all.

## `spindump` during the 45 % start

Not a hang report and nothing of `wsd`. macOS made a disk-writes report of its own `lsd` (Launch Services): `lsd_2026-11-23-224051…diag`, "2158.61 MB of
file backed memory dirtied over 36054 seconds … exceeding limit", 12:35:10 to 22:36:04; `spindump`'s own report (90 s of CPU in 107 s from 22:36:04.740) has all
its samples on behalf of pid 476, `symptomsd`. Ten hours of samples are written up in five minutes at half a core to most of one. What made `lsd` write:
each start replaced `wsd` in the app's bundle, rewrote its `Info.plist` and touched it; macOS registers such an app again and saves its whole
database, 11.7 MB: 182 of its 183 samples are that save, 21 on behalf of `wsd` processes by name and most others of processes that had ended (the real
starts' `wsd` 96412 and 5814 among them). A day of some 180 starts, ours, crossed the 2 GB macOS allows; the 45 % start was the one that did.
Measured (`proc_pid_rusage` of `lsd`): a bundle as `launch.sh` left it 11.7 to 11.8 MB a start, one left alone 0.
`launch.sh` now leaves a bundle that is what it has to be alone (Obelisk, `perf-timeline`): a dozen starts in a row wrote nothing. A player's few
starts a day never came near the limit; they save 12 MB of writes each.

## Why no window of the three starts was "frontmost"

Because the game was not: every look says Safari (pid 1184), and `wsd`'s log says `key=0 app_active=0 level=0` at every state, full screen included. macOS lets
no program take the front that the app in front neither started nor yields to; the session was started from a terminal service. The window was
shown all the same. The bench now opens the bundle of its own session's window server (as a click in the Dock), tells by name what was in front
instead, and stamps each window's cuts on the trace's clock. Reproduced on the owned client: in front with `open`, Safari without.
